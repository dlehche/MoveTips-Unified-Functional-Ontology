# Declarations

## Author contributions

Lei Che: conceptualization; original Human Function definition; UOHF
theory and ontology architecture; formal framework; methodology;
semantic and governance design; writing; revision; and final approval.
MoveTips Research & Engineering Team: engineering implementation
support, ontology-asset governance, and technical review.

## Funding

This formal framework paper was prepared within MoveTips Technology
(Beijing) Co., Ltd. No external grant support is claimed in this
document.

## Competing interests

Lei Che is the founder of the Human Function Framework and UOHF and is
affiliated with MoveTips Technology (Beijing) Co., Ltd., which develops
software, ontology assets, training, and services related to the
framework. This commercial relationship is disclosed as a potential
competing interest.

## Ethics statement

No human participants, identifiable personal data, clinical
intervention, or clinical trial were involved in the preparation of this
formal framework paper. Ethics approval was therefore not required for
this document.

## Data, ontology, and code availability

The designated public repository for versioned framework releases is the
Unified Ontology of Human Function repository:
https://github.com/dlehche/Unified-Ontology-Of-Human-Function. The
complete production ontology, governed relation topology, relation
contracts, implementation rules, and operational data assets are not
reproduced in full in this paper. Public release, mapping, and licensing
decisions are governed separately to preserve provenance, version
identity, safety constraints, and implementation integrity.

## Scope clarification

UOHF is a knowledge-representation, semantic-governance, research, and
decision-support framework. It is not an autonomous medical diagnosis
system, autonomous treatment system, completed whole-person
physiological simulator, or substitute for licensed professional
judgment. Disease diagnosis, medical restrictions, prescriptions,
surgery decisions, emergency assessment, and other regulated clinical
actions remain within the relevant professional and legal scope.
