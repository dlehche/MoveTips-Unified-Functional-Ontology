# 8. Problem Hypotheses and Evidence Status

$$
Z^{\mathrm{problem}}_{t}\subseteq\{Z_C,Z_A,Z_O\},
\qquad
S^{\mathrm{evidence}}_{t}\in
\{\mathrm{supported},\mathrm{weakened},\mathrm{contradicted},\mathrm{not\ assessed},\mathrm{insufficient},\mathrm{undetermined}\}.
$$

The framework uses two independent axes. The problem-hypothesis axis may
contain capacity-coverage failure (Z_C), functional-engagement
difficulty (Z_A), and other or residual explanation (Z_O), singly or
concurrently. Mixed presentation is therefore set membership in more
than one hypothesis class, not a fourth primitive problem type. The
evidence-status axis records supported, weakened, contradicted, not
assessed, insufficient, or undetermined. Evidence insufficiency is an
epistemic state, not a human-function problem.

## 8.1 Capacity evidence cannot be replaced by outcome evidence

A painful or unsuccessful task is not sufficient evidence that a
capacity is absent or impaired. The observed response may arise from an
excessive demand, environmental restriction, protective engagement,
medical pathology, fatigue, prior exposure, or measurement error. The
first formal claim should therefore be capacity-coverage failure under
the specified demand and conditions. A further claim of capacity
impairment or deficit requires independent evidence linked to the
capacity and an explicit reference or personal boundary.

## 8.2 Engagement evidence cannot be inferred from missing assertions

A Functional Engagement Assertion should record whether evidence
supports actual operation of a required human function through a
supported actual process. The assertion is reified because it is bound
to person, demand, function, context, time, evidence, provenance, and
rule version. A missing assertion is not a negative assertion. This rule
is required for open-world ontology semantics and for scientific honesty
when the relevant process was not observed or cannot yet be measured.

## 8.3 Compensation as a relation, not an automatic defect

Compensation is a change in how the body organizes a demand relative to
another available or expected route. It may be protective, necessary,
efficient, temporary, costly, or harmful depending on the person,
demand, context, boundary, and time. The framework therefore records
compensation and its consequences rather than declaring every deviation
from a reference pattern abnormal.

# 9. Result Non-Identifiability and Causal Hypotheses

## 9.1 Result Non-Identifiability Proposition

$$
e(X_1,a_1,d,k)=e(X_2,a_2,d,k)=y,
\qquad
(X_1,a_1)\ne(X_2,a_2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y\not\Rightarrow (X,a)_{\mathrm{unique}}.
$$
(4)

Proposition 2. If the response mapping e is non-injective over relevant
latent states and engagement processes, an observed response y does not
uniquely identify one underlying state-engagement configuration and
therefore cannot, by itself, establish one unique cause or limiting
factor.

Proof. Non-injectivity means that at least two distinct input pairs
(X1,a1) and (X2,a2) produce the same observed response y under the
specified demand and context. Therefore the inverse image of y contains
more than one state-engagement configuration. Because y is compatible
with more than one configuration, y alone cannot logically entail one
unique underlying state or engagement process; a unique causal
attribution requires additional evidence and an independently justified
causal model. □

Pain, instability, slowed performance, fatigue, and task failure are
common examples. The same knee pain during stair climbing may arise in
different persons from a recent load increase, reduced load tolerance,
local tissue sensitivity, a medical condition, fatigue, environmental
conditions, protective engagement, or combinations of these. The
proposition is not a weakness of UOHF. It is a formal statement of an
unavoidable inverse problem.

## 9.2 Belief state and evidence convergence

Because the true whole-person state and limiting factors are only
partially observable, the runtime model maintains an
evidence-conditioned belief state over plausible states and hypotheses.
Belief state is the general object; it is not defined as probability by
default.

$$
\Pi_t
=
\mathrm{BeliefState}\!\left(Y_{0:t},D_{0:t},K_{0:t},U_{0:t-1}\right)
$$ (5)

In the current research stage, Π is represented by governed statuses
such as supported, weakened, contradicted, not assessed, insufficient
evidence, and undetermined. A mature probabilistic specialization may
represent the belief state as a conditional probability distribution
over latent states and limiting-factor hypotheses, but only after
validated priors, likelihoods, dependencies, observation models, and
population calibration are available. Numerical percentages must not be
produced before those conditions are met.

## 9.3 Causal inference requires intervention or stronger design

A posterior ranking of hypotheses is not a proof of causation. Causal
claims require appropriate study design, temporal ordering, mechanistic
support, exclusion of alternatives, natural experiments, controlled
perturbations, or other valid causal methods [24]. An improvement
after one action supports—but does not uniquely prove—the original
hypothesis because natural fluctuation, regression to the mean,
co-interventions, expectancy, adaptation, and measurement variation may
also contribute.

## 9.4 Structure anchors are not diagnoses

A human-function capacity may depend on bodily structures and be
realized through bodily processes. A structure anchor can therefore
indicate where a capacity is realized, constrained, burdened, or
observed. It does not follow that the anchored structure is the unique
cause of a manifestation. The inferential chain must remain: demand →
required human function → engagement evidence → response and cost →
limiting-factor hypotheses → convergent assessment → primary problem. A
direct jump from pain location to structural pathology is prohibited
unless independently supported.
