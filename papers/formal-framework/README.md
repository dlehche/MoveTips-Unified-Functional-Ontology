## Core definition

> **Human function is the body's capacity to be appropriately engaged to meet internal and external demands.**

> **人体功能，就是身体被正常调用的能力。**

## Scope

This paper develops an implementation-oriented formal framework for human function within UOHF. It separates:

- whole-person state from evidence about that state;
- human-function capacity from engagement-pattern specifications;
- realizable and ex ante appropriate patterns from the actual engagement process;
- potential, predicted, and observed responses;
- problem hypotheses from their evidence status;
- structural, process, observation, constraint, and action anchors;
- governed action from subsequent change and reassessment.

Its central contribution is a demand-bounded realizability criterion and a governed reasoning architecture linking demand, capacity, functional engagement, response, evidence, hypotheses, admissible action, feedback, and longitudinal update.

The paper includes illustrative external-demand and internal-demand cases. These examples demonstrate governed reasoning and do not constitute diagnosis, treatment validation, or autonomous clinical decision making.

## Relationship to UOHF Definition 2.1

UOHF Definition 2.1 remains the current authoritative framework definition. This Version 1.0 formal-framework preprint develops a focused mathematical and implementation-oriented account of capacity, engagement, realizability, evidence, and action under that definition. It does not create a competing root definition or replace the version-governed framework publications listed in the repository's canonical publication map.

## Claim boundary

The paper does not claim:

- a completed whole-person physiological simulator;
- completed BFO conformance or OWL axiomatization;
- validated causal probabilities or an optimized treatment policy;
- autonomous diagnosis or treatment;
- clinical outcome validation;
- external expert consensus.
