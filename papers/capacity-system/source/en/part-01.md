## Abstract

The Unified Ontology of Human Function (UOHF) previously defined human function as the body's capacity to be appropriately engaged to meet internal and external demands and distinguished functional capacity from actual engagement, manifestations, measurements, processes, and inferences. Building on that framework, this paper publicly releases UOHF Version 1.0 as a citable catalogue of 18 core human functional capacities and 104 specific human functional capacities. The catalogue spans usable range, force output, load tolerance, postural control, movement organization, sensory-perceptual integration, sustained endurance, intake and swallowing, elimination and pelvic-floor control, recovery and adaptation, cognition and executive function, emotional and behavioral regulation, communication, internal physiological regulation, protection and defense, consciousness and sleep, sexual and reproductive function, and growth and maturation.

The catalogue was constructed through conceptual analysis, type-boundary audit, external calibration, source traceability, adjacent-capacity boundary audit, and completeness stress testing. Every capacity had to have a whole-person subject, a stable capacity identity, independent definability and evidence value, and a boundary separating it from structures, processes, tasks, episodic performances, measurements, and states. Every definition is linked to external scientific or professional sources. Those sources establish the provenance of relevant constructs, components, or boundaries; the wording of the definitions remains a UOHF ontological synthesis at the whole-person level rather than a quotation from any single source.

Version 1.0 is a versioned scientific coordinate system rather than an immutable claim of eternal exhaustiveness. The paper releases capacity names, hierarchy, definitions, key boundaries, and source bases, but not the complete demand-to-capacity matrix, structure and process realization networks, assessment or intervention mappings, person-specific reasoning rules, decision weights, or production data. # 1. Background and Aim
Knowledge of bodily structure, disease, physiological process, activity, participation, movement, psychology, and behavior is extensive, but it has historically been organized around disciplinary objects and professional tasks. For the same person, a shared coordinate is still needed to answer a simpler ontological question: what stable functional capacities does the human body have? UOHF places whole-person human functional capacities and their actual engagement under internal and external demands at the semantic center and explicitly separates capacities from structures, processes, measurements, symptoms, task outcomes, and professional judgments [1-3].

Existing international and disciplinary systems provide essential external reference points. The International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health provides a common vocabulary across body functions, activities, and participation [4,5], while exercise physiology, motor control, swallowing, continence, cognition, emotion, communication, sleep, pain, reproduction, growth, and repair provide mature constructs and evidence within their respective domains [6-35]. The present work does not rename those bodies of knowledge. It organizes selected constructs at a whole-person capacity level under a consistent type boundary.

The principal output is the public definition of 18 core and 104 specific human functional capacities in UOHF Version 1.0. Each definition is linked to explicit sources, and the paper also publishes the construction method, major adjacent-capacity boundaries, and a publication-version rule so that future research can audit why an object is treated as a capacity, why it is separated from its neighbors, and where its scientific provenance comes from.

# 2. Methods and Catalogue Construction
Catalogue construction followed general ontology-engineering principles of explicit scope, type separation, traceable provenance, and version governance [38-40], while the prior UOHF definition and formal framework supplied the upper-level semantic constraints [1-3]. Existing professional classifications were not simply concatenated. Candidate functions were first collected and then subjected to type audit, external calibration, boundary audit, and completeness stress testing.

## 2.1 Admission and Exclusion Rules
- Whole-person dependence: the object must be a stable functional potential of a specific person rather than a local function of an organ, tissue, cell, or molecule.

- Demand relevance: the capacity must explain how it supports an internal life-maintenance demand or an external real-world task demand.

- Capacity-process separation: a physiological process, movement occurrence, repair event, or behavioral episode is not itself the capacity; such events may realize, manifest, or provide evidence for a capacity.

- Capacity-measurement separation: angles, force values, scale scores, laboratory values, sleep duration, and similar observations are parameters or evidence rather than the capacity itself.

- Stable definability: a capacity must retain reusable semantics across episodes and support an independent evidence and judgment question.

- Auditable boundary: adjacent capacities must be distinguishable; one manifestation may involve multiple capacities and cannot justify automatic single-cause attribution.

## 2.1A Public “specific capacity” terminology and formal ontology type
For readability, this paper uses **104 specific human functional capacities** as a public umbrella expression. It is **not a new ontology type**. In governed UOHF capacity semantics, the 104 fine-grained objects are typed as either `SUBCAPACITY` or `CAPACITY_COMPONENT`; both are subordinate to their respective `CORE_CAPACITY` and must not be flattened into the same ontology level as the 18 core capacities.

Scholarly catalogue inclusion is also separate from production-runtime status: inclusion does not by itself assert that the corresponding CUI, lifecycle state, relation endpoints, assessment paths, or intervention paths are already released in production.

## 2.2 Candidate Collection, Type Audit, and Completeness Stress Testing
The candidate set was designed to cover stable functional contributions needed across internal life-maintenance demands and external real-world demands. Objects that described only anatomy, local biological processes, episodic events, tasks, demand parameters, states, outcomes, or organization of actual engagement were excluded. A candidate was retained only when it simultaneously satisfied whole-person subject identity, stable capacity status, independent evidence value, and an auditable boundary.

Completeness stress testing explicitly examined domains that are often underrepresented when human function is reduced to movement, including internal physiological regulation, consciousness and sleep, reproduction, tissue repair, and growth and maturation. The same process also tested the opposite error: duplicating fatigue, regulatory mechanisms, task actions, individual repair episodes, or a vague higher-order “overall coordination” as additional capacities. Catalogue expansion was therefore driven by the question of whether a distinct stable human functional capacity was missing, not by a target number or symmetric hierarchy.

The resulting 18 core and 104 specific capacities are therefore the Version 1.0 closure under the present admission rules rather than a preselected numerical target. Sustained Task Endurance Capacity intentionally has no forced child capacities in Version 1.0 because intensity, duration, frequency, pace, repetition, and task cost are better represented as demand conditions or evidence dimensions than renamed as separate stable capacities merely to create structural symmetry.

## 2.3 Source Traceability and External Calibration
Each definition is constrained by two source layers. UOHF publications provide the upper-level semantics of whole-person capacity, demand, and actual engagement [1-3]. External standards, textbooks, professional terminology systems, and peer-reviewed literature provide the relevant constructs, components, scientific mechanisms, or boundary evidence. A citation does not imply that the UOHF definition is textually identical to that source. It means that the corresponding scientific proposition is traceable to prior knowledge. For objects prone to category confusion, such as tissue repair, this paper uses both direct scientific literature and process-ontology sources to distinguish a stable repair capacity from an individual repair process [15,37].

## 2.4 Version 1.0 Publication Snapshot
This paper freezes the names, hierarchical placement, unified definitions, and source bindings of the 18 core and 104 specific capacities as the “UOHF Human Function Capacity System Version 1.0 publication snapshot.” Lifecycle states in later product implementations, databases, or research workflows do not retroactively alter this paper. Substantive additions, splits, merges, redefinitions, or retirements must be documented through a new public version rather than silently changing Version 1.0.

## 2.5 Separation of the scholarly catalogue from production runtime status
Version 1.0 freezes a public scholarly catalogue; it does not claim that the production database has simultaneously completed activation of every object, historical migration, relation-endpoint coverage, A8 assessment-evidence paths, A10 intervention mappings, or Reasoner wiring. Production systems remain separately governed by lifecycle, versioning, canonical resolution, relation payloads, evidence paths, and release gates.

# 4. Unified Definitions of the 18 Core Human Functional Capacities
## 1. C01 | Usable Range of Motion Capacity
**Definition:** The stable whole-person capacity, under specified conditions, to enter, traverse, control, and repeatedly use the bodily ranges required by internal or external demands while maintaining acceptable safety, cost, and task usability. [1,2,3,4,5,6,28]

## 2. C02 | Force Output Capacity
**Definition:** The stable whole-person capacity, under specified demands, to establish, regulate, transmit, and maintain force of the required direction, magnitude, and duration for propulsion, support, braking, or control of the body or external objects. [1,2,3,4,5,7,28]

## 3. C03 | Load-Bearing and Load-Tolerance Capacity
**Definition:** The stable whole-person capacity to bear, distribute, and transmit body weight, external load, repeated load, impact, and cumulative burden while keeping task cost and tissue risk within an acceptable boundary. [1,2,3,7,26,27]

## 4. C04 | Postural and Balance Control Capacity
**Definition:** The stable whole-person capacity to establish, maintain, and recover posture, support relationships, and control of the center of mass during stillness, movement, transitions, and external perturbations. [1,2,3,4,8,9,28]

## 5. C05 | Movement Organization and Coordination Capacity
**Definition:** The stable whole-person capacity to organize movement initiation, sequence, timing, rhythm, scaling, direction, multi-segment coordination, error correction, transition, and termination according to current demand. [1,2,3,4,5,28]
