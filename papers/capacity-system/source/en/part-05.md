# 6. Key Adjacent-Capacity Boundaries
A capacity catalogue cannot rely on names alone. The following boundaries address recurrent type confusions in Version 1.0. They are intended to prevent duplicate modeling and unsupported attribution from a single manifestation; they do not imply that adjacent capacities are physiologically independent.

| **Adjacent-capacity boundary**                                       | **Distinction rule**                                                                                                                                                                                                        | **Source basis** |
|----------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------|
| Usable range vs movement organization                                | Usable range asks whether the body has the range required by the demand; movement organization asks whether initiation, timing, direction, scaling, and multi-segment coordination can be organized within available range. | [4,5,28]       |
| Force output vs load tolerance                                       | Force output concerns active generation and regulation of force; load tolerance concerns bearing, distributing, and transmitting body weight, external load, impact, and cumulative mechanical burden.                      | [5,7,26,27]    |
| Load tolerance vs sustained endurance                                | Load tolerance emphasizes mechanical and tissue loading boundaries; sustained endurance emphasizes continued task performance across time and repetition with acceptable quality and cost.                                  | [5,6,26,27]    |
| Postural balance vs sensory integration                              | Postural balance concerns establishment and recovery of posture and center-of-mass control; sensory integration supplies body-environment information but is not itself postural control.                                   | [8,9,29,30]    |
| Sustained endurance vs internal physiological regulation             | Sustained endurance is a whole-person task-bearing capacity; internal physiological regulation supplies gas exchange, perfusion, energy availability, and other internal conditions that support it.                        | [5,6,19,31]    |
| Swallow-specific airway protection vs general protection and defense | Airway protection during swallowing is task-specific to intake and swallowing; general protection and defense spans broader threat response, recruitment, damage limitation, and termination.                               | [12,22,33]     |
| Elimination control vs internal physiological regulation             | Elimination control concerns sensation, storage, timing, initiation, coordination, continence, and emptying; fluid and electrolyte regulation is a physiological background rather than the same capacity.                  | [4,5,13,19]    |
| Internal physiological regulation vs recovery and adaptation         | Internal regulation maintains or restores current internal conditions; recovery and adaptation concerns return to usability after disturbance and longer-term adaptation to repeated demands.                               | [19,31]        |
| Cognitive-executive vs emotional-behavioral regulation               | Cognitive-executive capacity organizes comprehension, attention, memory, planning, judgment, inhibition, and switching; emotional-behavioral regulation organizes emotion, motivation, and goal-directed behavior.          | [5,16,17]      |
| Consciousness/sleep/arousal vs recovery                              | Sleep and arousal have independent timing and consciousness semantics; sleep can support recovery, but recovery is not identical to sleep.                                                                                  | [14,23,32]     |
| Sexual/reproductive vs elimination/pelvic-floor control              | Pelvic-floor and elimination control may support some sexual and reproductive tasks, but sexual response, reproductive cycling, fertility, gestation, childbirth, and lactation remain distinct.                            | [13,24,41]     |

# 7. Relationship to Existing Classifications and Disciplinary Knowledge
The UOHF capacity catalogue does not copy the ICF body-functions chapter into a second classification. The ICF supplies international reference points for many functional domains, while UOHF retypes candidate objects at the level of stable whole-person capacity and separates them from episodic processes, activities, task results, and measurements [4,5].

The catalogue is not bounded by the work objects of any single profession. Motor control, exercise physiology, swallowing, pelvic-floor science, cognition, psychology, communication, sleep, reproduction, growth, and repair are used as external scientific sources. They inform definitions without determining the upper-level organization of the whole-person catalogue.

Shared provenance does not imply semantic equivalence. The same external construct may enter UOHF research as a capacity source, process explanation, evidence source, or boundary calibration. The present paper therefore provides a source-traceable ontological synthesis rather than a mechanical mapping of external codes.

# 8. Completeness, Versioning, and Falsifiability
The Version 1.0 “18 + 104” catalogue is a provisional closure, not an eternal exhaustiveness claim. If future work identifies an internal or external demand that cannot be adequately represented by the current capacity set, or if a current capacity cannot support a distinguishable construct, reliable evidence, or stable prediction, the ontology must permit addition, splitting, merging, redefinition, or retirement.

The scientific value of the catalogue does not depend on the number of entries. It depends on testability: whether independent researchers can operationalize the same capacity consistently, whether capacity evidence under specified demands can explain or predict real-world performance, and whether longitudinal changes in capacity show stable and reproducible associations. A future Human Function Reference System must additionally establish reference ranges tied to population, condition, life stage, and measurement protocol.

# 9. Public Scope and Research Boundary
This paper releases capacity names, upper-level placement, unified definitions, source bases, and key adjacent-capacity boundaries as a citable public scientific coordinate system. It does not reproduce the complete demand-to-capacity requirement matrix, anatomical and physiological realization networks, assessment mappings, intervention mappings, person-specific reasoning rules, decision weights, production data structures, or real-user operational data. Those materials remain within separate research, validation, governance, or controlled implementation scopes.

This paper is not a clinical diagnostic standard, treatment guideline, or autonomous decision algorithm. No capacity definition alone converts a symptom, measurement value, or single task performance into a disease diagnosis, causal attribution, or specific intervention prescription.

# 11. Conclusion
UOHF Version 1.0 establishes a whole-person capacity coordinate system comprising 18 core and 104 specific human functional capacities across movement, physiology, cognition, behavior, communication, protection, reproduction, and the life course. Every definition is linked to traceable sources while preserving the distinction between external scientific provenance and UOHF ontological synthesis. The construction method shows that 18 + 104 is not a numerically engineered target but the Version 1.0 result of type exclusion, boundary audit, and completeness stress testing. The catalogue turns the question “what functional capacities does the human body have?” into a citable, measurable, comparable, falsifiable, and revisable scientific object.
